# v0.0.100 — Quotient refit fixes equivalence stability but misses the frozen m128 Q34 floor

## Purpose

v0.0.99 showed that the preregistered quotient point representation removes
the known signed-row / positive-column surface degrees of freedom before
training. v0.0.100 performs the one authorized matched refit.

The sealed v0.0.98 holdout is not accessed.

## Frozen matched experiment

Both representation families use the same support-only MLP:

`8 -> 16 -> 16 -> 1`, 433 learned scalars.

Matched conditions:

- OLD_CG5 vs QUOTIENT;
- same 48 opened v0.0.88 training sources;
- seeds 100001 / 100002;
- same initialization per seed;
- Adam 0.001, 12 epochs, fixed order;
- last epoch only;
- no validation selection or retry.

Evaluation is on 16 newly generated development bases, each paired with one
equivalent surface view:

- 8 m64 base + 8 m64 surface;
- 8 m128 base + 8 m128 surface.

This is development data, not a fresh confirmation holdout.

## Result

### Surface consistency

Matched OLD_CG5 refits preserve the exact top2m support between base and
equivalent surface view on:

- seed100001: 0/16 pairs
- seed100002: 0/16 pairs

QUOTIENT preserves it on:

- seed100001: **16/16**
- seed100002: **16/16**

So the representation-level equivalence fix survives training exactly under
this development audit.

### m64

Both quotient seeds clear Q34 in both m64 cells.

Seed100001:

- base utility 0.906888, burden 0.036572, complete/Direct 0.202569;
- surface utility 0.909211, burden 0.034290, complete/Direct 0.194065.

Seed100002:

- base utility 0.906577, burden 0.037370, complete/Direct 0.203503;
- surface utility 0.911908, burden 0.033768, complete/Direct 0.191238.

All m64 cases are fallback-free.

### m128

Both quotient seeds are economical and fully verified, but each has one
restricted-support miss out of eight and falls below the frozen 0.80 utility
floor.

Seed100001:

- base: 7/8 fallback-free, utility **0.787617**, burden 0.017061,
  complete/Direct 0.266149;
- surface: 7/8 fallback-free, utility **0.799773**, burden 0.015490,
  complete/Direct 0.249450.

Seed100002:

- base: 7/8 fallback-free, utility **0.796467**, burden 0.017051,
  complete/Direct 0.257896;
- surface: 7/8 fallback-free, utility **0.794749**, burden 0.015446,
  complete/Direct 0.254132.

No threshold is changed after observing these near misses.

### Combined 32 views

Both quotient seeds pass the aggregate Q34 arithmetic:

- seed100001 utility 0.807417, burden 0.018754, complete/Direct 0.250175;
- seed100002 utility 0.808900, burden 0.018750, complete/Direct 0.248795.

But the preregistration requires **every cell**, not only the aggregate, to
pass. Therefore the aggregate cannot rescue the failed m128 cells.

The matched OLD_CG5 controls show essentially the same m128 structural-recovery
boundary despite worse surface consistency. This is important: eliminating the
known surface nuisance changes invariance and slightly lowers proposal cost, but
does not by itself remove the rare m128 support miss.

## Decision

`STOP_QUOTIENT_REFIT_NO_FRESH_HOLDOUT`

- no second fit;
- no threshold rescue;
- no new fresh holdout;
- no reuse of the v0.0.98 holdout;
- no Q5 advance.

## Interpretation

v0.0.99–100 successfully separate two effects that were previously entangled.

1. **Surface representation defect:** real, and fixed. Quotient features reduce
   the known equivalence drift to numerical roundoff and the trained selector
   becomes exactly support-invariant on all 16 paired development problems.
2. **m128 structural recovery:** still incomplete. One 2m-support miss per
   eight cases is enough for a full Direct fallback to pull cell utility below
   the frozen 0.80 floor.

The next experiment must not train the model again. A higher-information next
question is whether the already-frozen quotient ranking contains the missed
structure at a modest larger support such as 4m. That can be tested as a
conditional fallback/authority ceiling before any additional learning.

If a small verifier-triggered support expansion cannot recover the misses
cheaply, stop this fixed point-support formulation and move one level up.
